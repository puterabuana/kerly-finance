The AI stock rally: valuation, earnings, and correction risk

The AI stock rally can be a structural trend, but prices that have run fast still have to be tested by earnings, margins, capex, and guidance. Here is the Kerly framework for separating quality growth from euphoria.

Updated Jul 5, 2026 7 min read By Equity Desk
In this brief The AI thesis Valuation Earnings Correction risk Sources

AI stocks can't be analyzed from the technology story alone. Investors need to see whether revenue is growing faster than expected, whether margins are holding, and whether capital spending is creating future cash flow rather than eroding returns.

The AI thesis: productivity or multiple expansion?

There are two main sources of higher AI stock prices. First, fundamental growth: revenue, backlog, margins, and cash flow improving on AI adoption. Second, multiple expansion: investors willing to pay a richer valuation because they believe the total addressable market will keep growing.

A healthy rally is usually driven by both, but not in equal proportion. When prices rise far faster than earnings revisions, correction risk increases because the market is already paying too much for growth that hasn't been proven.

How to read AI stock valuations

A high valuation doesn't automatically mean a bubble. For companies whose earnings and free cash flow are compounding quickly, a valuation premium can be reasonable. Problems appear when investors use the AI narrative to ignore revenue quality, customer concentration, competition, and capex needs.

  • Compare the forward P/E with expected earnings growth over the next 12-24 months.
  • Watch price-to-sales for companies whose net income is still small.
  • Test whether gross and operating margins rise alongside revenue.
  • Track analyst revisions after earnings, not just the one-day price reaction.
Kerly takeaway An AI rally is premium when earnings revisions rise with it. It is fragile when prices climb while guidance, margins, and cash flow fail to catch up with expectations.

The earnings that matter

During earnings season, three numbers often decide the market reaction: revenue growth, margins, and guidance. An EPS beat driven by buybacks or cost cuts can matter less than guidance that signals softening demand.

For AI stocks, investors also need to read capex. High capex can be bullish if it builds capacity for real demand. But overly aggressive capex can weigh on free cash flow and raise questions about return on invested capital.

When does correction risk rise?

Correction risk rises when bond yields climb, the dollar strengthens, market breadth narrows, and investors start selling winners to lock in profits. A correction can also follow strong earnings if expectations were already too high.

Signals to watch: falling EPS revisions, distribution volume in the leaders, widening credit spreads, and rotation into defensives. When they occur together, the market is usually cutting risk duration.

Research sources

  • SEC EDGAR for reading public companies' 10-K, 10-Q, and 8-K filings.
  • BEA GDP for the context of US economic growth.
  • Federal Reserve Financial Stability Report for reading systemic risk.

FAQ

Are AI stocks still attractive after a big rally?

They can be, if earnings revisions and free cash flow are still catching up with the price. But investors should be more selective on valuation and the quality of guidance.

What signals that an AI rally is overheating?

Early signs are prices rising without earnings confirmation, investors chasing low-quality secondary names, and breadth weakening while the index still climbs.
